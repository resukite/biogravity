## Executive Abstract & Empirical Context

The interaction of strong ($a_0&gt;1$), superluminal X-mode electromagnetic waves with magnetized pair plasma falls into two regimes set by $\varepsilon_{\rm B}\equiv a_0ω_{\rm B}/ω_0$, the ratio of the wave strength parameter to its frequency, in units of cyclotron frequency. For $\varepsilon_{\rm B}&lt;1$ the wave propagates until induced scattering disrupts it. Using analytical theory and kinetic simulations, we show that the background magnetic field introduces a restoring frequency $Ω_{\rm B}$ to scattering; when $Ω_{\rm B}$ exceeds the scattering rate, the unattenuated length grows from $\simeq\varepsilon_{\rm p}^{-2/3}$ to $\simeq\varepsilon_{\rm B}^{1/2}/\varepsilon_{\rm p}$ wavelengths ($\varepsilon_{\rm p}\equiv a_0ω_{\rm p}/ω_0$; $ω_{\rm p}$ is the plasma frequency). When $a_0^2&gt;\varepsilon_{\rm B}&gt;1$, the plasma current is nonlinear, and the wave is damped while riding a motional electric field, so one half-cycle appears attenuated. This framework has implications for the duration of fast radio bursts and whether they can escape their surroundings.




  Investigating these dynamics within low Earth orbit laboratories provides an unprecedented analytical window. By dampening terrestrial buoyant convection and hydrostatic pressure heads, biological macromolecules demonstrate non-classical equilibrium regimes that cannot be resolved in ground-based centrifuge simulators.
