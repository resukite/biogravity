Core Finding & Direct Answer (TL;DR)

Tardigrades survive the vacuum of space by entering an extreme metabolic suspension called anhydrobiosis (the 'tun' state). They expel 97% of body water, replace it with tardigrade-specific intrinsically disordered proteins (TDPs) that vitrify into protective bioglass, and deploy a unique protein called Dsup (Damage suppressor) that physically wraps around DNA to shield it from lethal cosmic radiation.

Pedagogical Abstract & Concept Overview

Student & Researcher Key Takeaways
  • ✓ In 2007, the European Space Agency's FOTON-M3 mission exposed desiccated tardigrades directly to open space vacuum and cosmic radiation; many survived and later reproduced.
  • ✓ Tardigrades survive dehydration via anhydrobiosis: they expel over 97% of cellular water, curling into a dense metabolic stasis capsule called a 'tun'.
  • ✓ During desiccation, tardigrade-specific intrinsically disordered proteins (TDPs) form a protective biological glass (vitrification), locking organelles and enzymes in place.
  • ✓ A unique nucleosome-binding protein called Dsup (Damage suppressor) physically binds to chromatin, shielding DNA strands from oxidative stress and ionizing radiation.

11. The FOTON-M3 Experiment: Real Life in the Vacuum of Orbit

In September 2007, the European Space Agency launched the TARDIS (Tardigrades in Space) experiment aboard the uncrewed Russian satellite FOTON-M3. Two species of microscopic ecdysozoans (Richtersius coronifer and Milnesium tardigradum) were mounted on an external payload pallet and exposed directly to open space for 10 days in low Earth orbit.

The conditions were brutal: zero atmospheric pressure ($10^{-6}\text{ to } 10^{-4}\text{ Pa}$), temperatures plunging toward $-196^\circ\text{C}$, and unfiltered cosmic vacuum ultraviolet (UV) radiation exceeding $7,000\text{ kJ/m}^2$.

Upon return to Earth and rehydration in sterile spring water, over 68% of vacuum-exposed tardigrades reanimated within 30 minutes, crawled, laid viable eggs, and produced healthy offspring—marking the first time complex multicellular animals survived unprotected orbital space exposure.

22. The Biochemical Weaponry: Vitrification and the Dsup Shield

How does an animal survive conditions that disintegrate human tissue? The molecular secret involves three non-negotiable biochemical mechanisms:

  • Intrinsically Disordered Proteins (TDPs) & Vitrification: When water leaves the tardigrade cell, unstructured cytoplasmic proteins solidify into an amorphous biological glass (vitrification). This halts all metabolic activity ($< 0.01\%$ of baseline) and immobilizes delicate protein machinery so enzymes cannot denature or aggregate.
  • Dsup (Damage Suppressor Protein): Discovered in the genome of Ramazzottius varieornatus, Dsup is an exceptionally basic protein that electrostatically coats genomic chromatin like an armor sleeve. When ionizing cosmic radiation generates free hydroxyl radicals ($\cdot OH$), Dsup absorbs the oxidative shock, suppressing DNA double-strand breaks by over 40%.
  • Hyperactive DNA Repair: Upon rehydration, the tardigrade upregulates DNA repair ligases and polymerases, rapidly stitching broken chromosomal fragments back together within hours.

Google Search & Featured Snippets FAQ

Can tardigrades live on the Moon right now? ▾

In 2019, the Israeli lunar lander Beresheet crashed onto the lunar surface carrying thousands of dehydrated tardigrades in epoxy resin. While their desiccated tun states could theoretically survive the Moon's vacuum and cold, they cannot wake up, move, or reproduce without liquid water, oxygen, and nutrients.

Are tardigrades alien lifeforms? ▾

No. Genetic sequencing has decisively shown that tardigrades are terrestrial invertebrates belonging to the superphylum Ecdysozoa, closely related to arthropods (insects, crustaceans) and nematodes.

Can humans use the Dsup protein? ▾

Yes, in laboratory studies! In 2016, researchers at the University of Tokyo inserted the tardigrade Dsup gene into cultured human kidney cells. The engineered human cells experienced roughly 40% less DNA fragmentation when bombarded with lethal X-ray radiation.
