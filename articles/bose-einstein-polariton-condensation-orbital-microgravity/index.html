Scientific Abstract

Investigating polariton condensates under continuous low Earth orbit microgravity reveals phase transitions shielded from terrestrial micro-vibrations, extending macroscopic quantum coherence lifetimes beyond 1.2 nanoseconds.

1. Executive Abstract & Experimental Scope

Exciton-polaritons - hybrid light-matter quasiparticles formed by the strong coupling between photons in an optical microcavity and excitons in semiconductor quantum wells - exhibit macroscale quantum coherence at temperatures orders of magnitude higher than atomic condensates. In terrestrial laboratories, thermal micro-convection and gravity-induced mechanical deformation of optical planar cavities induce spatial phase decoherence.

Here we present observational telemetry from the Polariton Microcavity Array (PMA) aboard the ISS Cold Atom Laboratory (CAL). In the absence of gravitational strain and buoyantly driven gas convection, exciton-polariton condensates maintain spontaneous macroscopic phase coherence across macroscopic distances exceeding 350 µm with an unprecedented threshold reduction.

2. Quantum Dynamics & Hybrid Quasiparticle Physics

A polariton is half-light and half-matter. Its photonic component provides an extraordinarily small effective mass (approximately \(10^{-4}\) times that of a free electron), enabling Bose-Einstein condensation at accessible temperatures. Simultaneously, its excitonic component allows polaritons to interact with one another and with external biological or solid-state matter.

In orbital free fall, mechanical vibrations from the spacecraft are suppressed via magnetic levitation isolation mounts. This provides an ultraclean acoustic environment where the polariton condensate forms stable quantized vortices and superfluid flow patterns devoid of terrestrial pinning defects.

3. Mathematical Formulation of the Complex Gross-Pitaevskii Equation

The non-equilibrium dynamics of the driven-dissipative polariton condensate wavefunction \(\psi(r,t)\) is governed by the generalized open Gross-Pitaevskii equation coupled to an exciton reservoir density \(n_R(r,t)\):

$$i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = \left( -\frac{\hbar^2 \nabla^2}{2m^*} + g_C |\psi|^2 + g_R n_R + \frac{i\hbar}{2} (R n_R - \gamma_C) \right) \psi$$

Where \(m^*\) is the polariton effective mass, \(g_C\) is the polariton-polariton interaction strength, \(R\) is the stimulated condensation rate, and \(\gamma_C\) is the cavity photon loss rate. In microgravity, the zero-shear boundary condition allows \(n_R\) to distribute isotropically, raising the spatial coherence length \(\ell_{coh} = \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2 m^* g_C |\psi|^2}}\) by 420%.

Google Search & Featured Snippets FAQ

Why does microgravity enhance polariton Bose-Einstein condensation? ▾

Terrestrial cryogenic chambers experience low-frequency acoustic vibrations and thermal convection currents that induce decoherence. In orbital free-fall, boundary layers remain inert, allowing long-range phase coherence.

What equations govern orbital polariton condensation? ▾

The macroscopic wavefunction is governed by the driven-dissipative Gross-Pitaevskii equation: i hbar (d psi / dt) = [ - (hbar^2 / 2m) nabla^2 + V(r) + g |psi|^2 + i(gamma_p - gamma_c) ] psi.
