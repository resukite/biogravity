## 1. Executive Abstract & Empirical Context

  
We examine the conditions under which quantum parameter estimation is compatible with the concept of a ``true'' quantity value: a unique value of the estimated quantity that exists prior to and independently of measurement. While quantum parameter estimation typically assumes that the quantity to be estimated is a parameter, we argue that this assumption can be lifted, opening the way to a more general formulation of estimation that may apply even in situations where the quantity to be estimated is no longer a parameter, but instead a quantum variable susceptible to quantum fluctuations. We identify three assumptions that characterize the way estimation is often presented in the quantum metrology literature, namely, as a procedure for inferring an unknown ``true value&quot;, which is assumed to be unique and to exist prior to and independently of measurement. Using our more general framework, we prove a no-go theorem showing that the preceding assumptions are jointly incompatible with two further assumptions: the completeness of the quantum state (no `hidden variables'), and the presence of entanglement between the probe systems and the system under measurement. We note that the presence of such entanglement is a generic prediction of standard quantum theory. Consequently, if one is not willing to accept hidden variables, nor willing to modify standard quantum theory by introducing a new physical mechanism that destroys entanglement at some scale or regime, then a realist account of estimation is ultimately impossible.


Investigating these dynamics within low Earth orbit laboratories provides an unprecedented analytical window. By dampening terrestrial buoyant convection and hydrostatic pressure heads, biological macromolecules demonstrate non-classical equilibrium regimes that cannot be resolved in ground-based centrifuge simulators.
