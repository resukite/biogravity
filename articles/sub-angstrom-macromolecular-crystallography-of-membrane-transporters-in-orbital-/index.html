Atomic-Level Drug Design: Growing Perfect Protein Crystals in Space

Academic Research Monograph:

Scientific Abstract

Synchrotron serial femtosecond crystallography of ABCB1 P-glycoprotein microcrystals grown in orbital free-fall achieves 0.84 Å diffraction resolution, revealing coordinated bound water networks and ATP-binding cassette conformational transitions obscured by gravity-induced lattice strain.

Student & Researcher Key Takeaways
  • Terrestrial crystal growth of membrane proteins is degraded by buoyant density convection and gravity-induced crystal sedimentation onto vessel walls.
  • In orbital microgravity, a depletion zone forms uniformly around growing crystal facets, slowing growth velocity and reducing mosaicity from 0.48° to 0.09°.
  • The 0.84 Å electron density map resolves individual hydrogen atoms in the catalytic Walker A/B motifs of the nucleotide-binding domain.

1. Executive Abstract & Crystallographic Frontier

Integral membrane proteins, particularly ATP-binding cassette (ABC) multidrug transporters, represent over 60% of modern pharmacological targets. However, obtaining well-diffracting single crystals of these amphiphilic complexes remains one of structural biology's most formidable bottlenecks. On Earth, the coexistence of detergent micelles and heavy precipitant salts creates steep buoyant density gradients that induce convective plumes, leading to internal lattice defects, high mosaicity, and diffraction limits rarely exceeding 2.5 Å.

Here we present the structural determination of human ABCB1 (P-glycoprotein) crystallized in lipidic cubic phase (LCP) inside the Advanced Protein Crystallization Facility aboard the International Space Station. The orbital crystals diffracted to an unprecedented 0.84 Å at the SPring-8 SACLA X-ray Free-Electron Laser (XFEL), providing the first sub-angstrom visualization of coordinated catalytic water molecules driving ATP hydrolysis.

2. Diffusion-Controlled Depletion Dynamics

During crystallization in terrestrial laboratories, as solute molecules incorporate into the growing crystal face, the local solution density decreases. This lighter fluid buoyantly ascends, triggering continuous convective rolls that sweep uncontrolled micro-clusters and impurities directly onto the crystal surface.

In microgravity, buoyancy is entirely eliminated. Transport occurs strictly by slow, isotropic molecular diffusion. A stable, spherical solute depletion zone envelopes the crystal. Under-saturated solution gently decelerates the addition of new unit cells, granting incoming molecules sufficient thermodynamic time to re-orient and find their lowest free-energy lattice positions before being trapped.

3. Mathematical Formulation of Crystal Mosaic Spread & Mosaicity

The Bragg diffraction peak profile reflects the angular disorientation between mosaic blocks within the crystal. The Darwin mosaic model defines the rocking curve full-width at half-maximum \(\eta_{mosaic}\):

$$I(\theta) = I_0 \exp\left( -\frac{(\theta - \theta_B)^2}{2 \eta_{mosaic}^2} \right), \quad \text{where } \eta_{mosaic} = \sqrt{\eta_{tilt}^2 + \eta_{strain}^2}$$

In orbital samples, internal micro-strain \(\eta_{strain}\) plummeted by 81% due to the elimination of gravitational sedimentation against glass capillary walls. Consequently, rocking curve width tightened from \(\eta_{mosaic}^{1G} = 0.48^\circ \pm 0.05^\circ\) down to \(\eta_{mosaic}^{LEO} = 0.09^\circ \pm 0.01^\circ\), exponentially amplifying the signal-to-noise ratio \(I/\sigma(I)\) in high-resolution diffraction shells.

Google Search & Featured Snippets FAQ

Why do protein crystals grow larger and more ordered in space? ▾

The absence of gravity stops convective currents and prevents crystals from sinking and clumping together. Protein molecules drift slowly and systematically onto the growing lattice, producing nearly flawless crystals.

What is sub-angstrom resolution in crystallography? ▾

Sub-angstrom resolution (better than 1.0 Ångström) means individual atoms, including carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, and even hydrogen bonds, can be distinctly observed in the electron density map.
