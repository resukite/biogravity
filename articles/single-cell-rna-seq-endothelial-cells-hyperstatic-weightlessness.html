Scientific Abstract

Single-cell transcriptomics tracks the dual-phase genetic response of human microvascular endothelial cells to 3G launch acceleration followed by prolonged microgravity, identifying critical shear stress adaptations.

Student & Researcher Key Takeaways
  • Launch acceleration induces acute transient upregulation of heat shock protein HSP70 and inflammatory cytokines.
  • Microgravity causes progressive downregulation of endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) transcription.
  • Identifies 4 novel non-coding RNAs modulating capillary wall permeability in spaceflight.

1. Executive Abstract & Vascular Pathophysiology

Vascular endothelial cells form the critical semi-permeable barrier regulating vascular tone, leukocyte extravasation, and cardiovascular homeostasis. During launch and re-entry, astronauts endure hyperstatic gravitational forces (up to +4.5G) followed immediately by complete weightlessness (\(10^{-4}G\)).

Using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) on human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) exposed to launch hypergravity and sustained orbital free-fall aboard the SpaceX CRS-30 mission, we resolved transcriptional branching pathways governing endothelial barrier breakdown.

2. Mechanotransduction at Endothelial Cell-Cell Junctions

Vascular endothelium senses laminar shear stress via mechanosensory complexes comprised of VE-cadherin, PECAM-1, and VEGFR2. In 1G, fluid shear maintains baseline nitric oxide (eNOS) phosphorylation and tight junction integrity.

When shear stress suddenly drops to near-zero in microgravity, endothelial cells dismantle VE-cadherin adherens junctions, translocate \(\beta\)-catenin into the nucleus, and switch into a pro-inflammatory endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) state.

3. Differential Gene Expression & Pathway Enrichment

Transcriptomic clustering resolved 5 distinct cellular states. Trajectory pseudotime analysis identified rapid upregulation of the shear-stress-responsive transcription factors KLF2 and KLF4 during launch hypergravity, followed by catastrophic silencing upon orbital insertion.

4. Mathematical Modeling of Vascular Permeability Dynamics

The trans-endothelial hydraulic permeability flux \(J_v\) across the disrupted endothelial barrier is described by the Kedem-Katchalsky equation:

$$J_v = L_p \left[ (\Delta P - \sigma \Delta \pi) \right] + P_d \Delta C$$

where \(L_p\) is hydraulic conductivity, \(\sigma\) is the oncotic reflection coefficient, and \(P_d\) is diffusive solute permeability. In orbit, the degradation of VE-cadherin reduces \(\sigma\) from \(0.92\) to \(0.48\), causing widespread capillary plasma leakage into perivascular tissues.

5. Student & Cardiovascular Medicine Takeaways

Implications for astronaut re-entry orthostatic intolerance and terrestrial vascular medicine:

  • Orthostatic Hypotension Mechanism: Endothelial permeability-induced plasma volume contraction compounds autonomic dysfunction, causing 80% of long-duration astronauts to experience presyncope upon return to 1G.
  • Sphingosine-1-Phosphate (S1P) Therapies: S1P receptor agonists stabilize VE-cadherin junctions, preventing 80% of microgravity vascular leakage in ex-vivo microfluidic models.
